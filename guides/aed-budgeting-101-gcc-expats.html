Budgeting basics

AED Budgeting 101: A Simple System for GCC Expats

Bramora Labs · Updated August 26, 2026 · 6 min read
Short answer

Start from your net salary in AED, set aside rent and fixed bills first (they're usually the largest and most rigid), give remittances their own line rather than folding them into savings, and only then split what's left between daily spending and true savings. The categories that matter here differ from a generic budgeting template — rent is typically annual or quarterly, and remittances are often the second-biggest expense.

Start with what's different about GCC budgeting

  • Rent is often paid in one to four cheques a year, not monthly — which means it doesn't show up as a monthly expense unless you deliberately divide it and set the portion aside each month.
  • No income tax on salary anywhere in the GCC, so take-home pay is closer to gross than expats from most other countries are used to — easy to mistake for more disposable income than it is once savings and remittances are accounted for.
  • Remittances are often a fixed, recurring commitment, not a leftover you send when there's extra — worth budgeting for up front, the same way you'd budget for rent.
  • School fees, if you have children, are typically a large annual or termly cost, similar to rent in how it should be divided monthly for budgeting purposes.

A starting split

This isn't a rigid formula — it's a starting point to adjust to your actual numbers:

CategorySuggested shareNotes
Rent & fixed bills~35-40%Divide annual/quarterly rent cheques into a monthly amount, don't budget only for the months a cheque is due
Remittances~10-20%Give this its own category — see our remittance tracking guide
Savings~15-20%Separate from remittances — money sent home isn't the same as money saved
Daily spendingRemainderGroceries, transport, dining, everything variable

Set these up before your first full month

  1. An account (or sub-budget) for the portion of annual rent you're setting aside monthly.
  2. A dedicated remittance category, logged in the currency you actually send in.
  3. A separate savings category — don't let it get absorbed into "leftover money," which tends to be zero.
  4. If Zakat applies to you, a note of when your zakat year starts, so you're not calculating it from scratch every time.
