Zakat

How to Calculate Zakat on Savings and Salary

Bramora Labs · Updated August 26, 2026 · 6 min read
Short answer

Zakat is 2.5% of your net zakatable wealth — cash, gold, silver, investments, and money owed to you, minus debts you owe — provided that total is at or above the nisab threshold (roughly the value of 85g of gold or 595g of silver) and has been held for one lunar year. Salary isn't taxed as it arrives; it becomes zakatable once it accumulates into savings above nisab.

This is a practical, non-religious guide to the calculation. It is not a fatwa. Zakat rulings differ between scholars and schools of thought — for your exact obligation, confirm with a qualified scholar.

The four things you need

  1. Your zakatable wealth — see the list below.
  2. Your debts — amounts you owe that are deductible.
  3. The current gold or silver price — to work out nisab in your local currency.
  4. Whether a lunar year has passed — Zakat is due on wealth held for roughly a Hijri year, not a snapshot of today's balance alone.

What counts as zakatable wealth

IncludeExclude
Cash on hand and in bank accountsYour home (primary residence)
Gold and silver you ownYour car
Investments (stocks, funds, business assets)Personal-use items — furniture, electronics, clothing
Money owed to you that you expect to recoverWealth held for less than a lunar year

What you can deduct

Subtract debts genuinely due — money you owe and are expected to repay in the near term. What remains is your net zakatable wealth.

The nisab threshold

You only owe Zakat if your net zakatable wealth is at or above nisab. Two standards are commonly used:

StandardWeightNotes
Gold~85g (some scholars: up to 87.48g)Higher value, fewer people meet it
Silver~595g (some scholars: up to 612.36g)Lower value — many scholars recommend it, since more people then qualify to give, meaning more Zakat reaches those in need

Take the weight, multiply by today's price per gram in your currency, and that's your nisab value. If your net zakatable wealth is at or above it, Zakat is due.

Worked example

Say someone in the UAE has:

  • AED 45,000 in savings
  • AED 8,000 worth of gold jewelry held as savings (not everyday personal wear, per their own scholar's guidance)
  • AED 3,000 owed to them by a friend, expected back
  • AED 6,000 in credit card and personal debt

Net zakatable wealth: 45,000 + 8,000 + 3,000 − 6,000 = AED 50,000.

Using the silver standard, illustratively at AED 3.00/gram: nisab = 595 × 3.00 = AED 1,785. (Check today's actual silver or gold price — it moves daily.) Since AED 50,000 is well above AED 1,785, Zakat is due:

50,000 × 2.5% = AED 1,250.

Frequently asked

What percentage is Zakat on savings?

2.5% (one fortieth) of net zakatable wealth, once it's at or above nisab and held for a lunar year.

What is nisab?

The minimum wealth threshold before Zakat applies — commonly the value of ~85g of gold or ~595g of silver at current prices (figures vary slightly by scholar).

Gold or silver nisab — which should I use?

Both are valid; it's a choice between you and your scholar. The silver nisab is lower, so many scholars recommend it — more people end up qualifying to give.

Do I pay Zakat directly on my salary?

No — Zakat applies to wealth you hold and retain above nisab for a lunar year, not to income the moment it's earned.
