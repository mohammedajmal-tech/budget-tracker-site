Budgeting basics

How Much Should You Save From Your Salary in the UAE/GCC?

Bramora Labs · Updated August 30, 2026 · 6 min read
Short answer

A reasonable starting target is 20-30% of net income — higher than the ~20% often quoted elsewhere, since GCC salaries are typically paid without income tax. But the number that actually matters is what's left after rent (often billed annually or quarterly, not monthly) and remittances, which for many expats is a fixed, non-negotiable line item rather than a leftover.

Why the generic 50/30/20 rule doesn't quite fit here

The popular 50/30/20 split — 50% needs, 30% wants, 20% savings — was built around a monthly-rent, single-currency, taxed-income budget. Three things break that model for a typical GCC expat:

  • Rent isn't monthly. It's commonly paid in one to four cheques a year, so a monthly figure only appears if you deliberately divide it and set the portion aside — otherwise it distorts your monthly "needs" number entirely.
  • Take-home pay looks larger than it behaves. No income tax on salary anywhere in the GCC means net pay is close to gross, which can create a false sense of headroom before rent, remittances and savings are accounted for.
  • Remittances don't map to "wants" or "needs." For many expats, money sent home is a fixed, recurring commitment — closer to a bill than a discretionary want — but it's rarely built into a generic budgeting template as its own category.

These numbers won't fit everyone — someone supporting a family back home may send far more than 20%, and someone with employer-provided housing may have little to no rent line at all. Treat this as a starting split to adjust against your real numbers, not a rule.

Why remittances shouldn't count as savings

It's tempting to lump remittances into your savings percentage, especially if the money is going toward a goal back home — a house, a parent's expenses, a sibling's education. But it's spent, not saved: it's left your control and your account. Counting it as savings overstates how much you're actually retaining for yourself, and makes it harder to plan for your own goals separately from the ones you're funding elsewhere.

How to find your actual number

  1. Track a full month of real spending before picking a target — a percentage based on a guess is worse than one based on your actual rent, remittance and bill totals.
  2. Separate rent, remittances, savings and daily spending into distinct categories, not one combined "expenses" bucket.
  3. Set the savings percentage last, as whatever's genuinely left over — not first, as an aspirational number you then have to force the rest of the budget to fit.

Frequently asked

What percentage of my salary should I save in the UAE/GCC?

A starting point of 20-30% of net income, adjusted for your actual rent and remittance commitments — higher than the generic 20% often quoted, reflecting tax-free pay.

Does the 50/30/20 rule work here?

Not cleanly — it assumes monthly rent, no remittances, and taxed income, none of which match a typical GCC expat budget.

Should remittances count toward my savings percentage?

No — track them as their own category. They're spent, not saved, even when they're going toward a goal.
